Week of November 22, 2026

I Was Sick, and Ye Visited Me

“I was sick, and ye visited me.”

Matthew 25:36, KJV1

When someone is sick, it’s easy to say, “Let me know if you need anything.” That’s a kind offer, but it leaves the next step with the person who may already feel like a burden. In Matthew 25, Jesus describes someone taking that step. The righteous ask when they saw Him sick and came to Him. Jesus answers that what they did for the least of His brethren, they did for Him.

In his 1786 sermon On Visiting the Sick, John Wesley asked what a Christian with little money could give. His answer included something within reach of anyone who could spare the time: pray for the sick and pray with them. Wesley also urged people who could make time regularly to set aside a specific hour for visiting.2

Think of someone who’s been home sick, recovering from surgery, or caring for a loved one. Instead of waiting for them to ask, offer something specific. “Could I drop off dinner Tuesday?” or “Would you have time for a call this evening?” And if they welcome prayer, take a moment to pray with them before you go.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, bring to mind someone who could use a visit this week. Help me make time, listen patiently, and offer what they actually need. Give them strength and comfort today. In Jesus' name, amen.

Sources

  2. John Wesley, “On Visiting the Sick” (1786), sermon 98, part III, paragraphs 3–4, Wesley Corpus. ↩
